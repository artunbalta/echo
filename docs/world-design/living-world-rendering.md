# Living world rendering

The September reference moves the visual direction toward daylight, soft terrain, rounded natural forms, dense wind-driven vegetation and an articulated third-person body. Geography is still the existing seeded spherical planet. No parcel was reassigned and no coastline or terrain seed was changed.

`parcelEnvironmentLayers` is the shared environment factory. The landing selection mounts it in `ParcelPreview` with input disabled; the playable route adds measurement and optional demo stations. Preview construction never creates a measurement chain. The selected H3 cell and manifest are passed directly to `/parcel/[cell]`. Camera placement and spawn resolution are shared. The preview is a real scene, rather than an illustration standing in for a parcel.

The ground interpolates its existing lattice normals. The sky has a daytime starting hour and procedural drifting clouds, with the existing day cycle and directional shadow light. Globe atmosphere is cosmetic and does not participate in parcel picking.

Detailed vegetation is a separate rendering layer. It uses the scatter layer's exact matrices and never modifies colliders, placement, far cards or shadow buffers. It restores the original main buffer before the next cull, then partitions visible instances into detail and ordinary meshes. Trees retain detail to 180 m; larger rocks and shrubs to 75 m; small vegetation to 34 m. A complementary screen-door transition blends both forms around each boundary. Local-space, derivative-filtered surface grain adds bark striation and foliage/stone colour variation; it follows the geometry during wind animation. Distant and shadow geometry remains inexpensive. Actual full-frame cost includes these new draws; the earlier scatter-only budget is not a measurement of this entire scene.

Ground cover streams 8 m patches around the player. A parcel/seed/patch hash determines the same blades on every visit. Only suitable land biomes grow them; water is excluded. At most one patch is constructed per frame, old patches are disposed, and an outer distance fade avoids a hard cut. GPU wind and a proximity wake bend the grass without changing collision. This is a visual response, not persistent flattening or a vegetation physics solver.

The shared avatar now has shoulder/elbow/wrist and hip/knee/ankle bone chains. Four weighted skinned meshes span the limbs, so bending joints deforms a continuous skin. A two-bone foot solver keeps soles level during stance, blends running elbow flexion and adds airborne poses. Terrain samples adjust the feet within the leg's reach. Parcel walking and running now use 1.65 m/s and 4.2 m/s, with acceleration and braking in metres; the default profile for legacy/networked hosts stays unchanged. Linear backward foot travel during stance matches these speeds. The existing jump trajectory, gameplay activities and obstacle collision remain authoritative. When movement presses against a reachable solid, a two-bone arm solver braces the hand against its collider surface and blends out on release. The follow camera also intersects expanded prop cylinders analytically, including thin trunks. Full-body contact IK, authored motion capture, root-motion matching, physically simulated cloth and arbitrary mesh-surface hand contact are not implemented. The locomotion measurement sampler retains its legacy speed normalization; its frozen model has not been recalibrated for the new parcel speeds.

The planet still loads a bounded region of the selected address. It is not a seamless planet-wide streaming client. Overlapping regions retain the existing local scatter generation; this change guarantees preview/entry agreement for an address, not canonical cross-address vegetation persistence. These are separate world-model extensions, and should not be inferred from the visual changes.

Validation includes 184 passing web tests and the shared planet suite (121 passing, four skipped), including globe/ground agreement, layer composition, bone hierarchy and skin weights, foot IK reconstruction and stance velocity, metre-scale movement, reachable hand targets, camera cylinders, detail matrix preservation, overlap coverage and buffer restoration. The web TypeScript check passes. Live browser checks exercised landing selection/entry and rainforest, savanna and grassland scenes without browser errors. A development rainforest view ran around 120 FPS at 628,415 triangles and 70 draw calls on this machine; this is a single-view observation, not a mobile or production performance guarantee. Development canvas hosts expose frame rate, triangle count and draw calls as data attributes, without adding a production HUD.
