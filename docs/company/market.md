# Capital outruns evidence in human simulation

*A market and competitor map for ECHO, dated 5 November 2026. Research carried out 5–6 November 2026 from the notes in "ECHO market and competitor map". Confidence labels from the notes are kept: "company-reported", "investor-reported", "aggregator-only" (Crunchbase, PitchBook, Dealroom and similar), "tracker estimate", "search-excerpt-only" (the page itself was not read), "headline-only", "Wikipedia-sourced" and UNVERIFIED.*

As of 5 November 2026, the market ECHO would enter has plenty of money and little proof. Simile has raised about **$300M at a $2B valuation** ([Business Insider via Yahoo Finance, 27 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-startup-raised-300-million-040101685.html)). Aaru sold equity at a **$1B headline price**, while its lead investor bought most of its stake at **$450M** ([TechCrunch, 3 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/03/why-ai-startups-are-selling-the-same-equity-at-two-different-prices/)). Salesforce closed its purchase of the AI-interview platform Listen Labs on 2 November 2026, at a reported **~$2B** ([Salesforce, 29 Sep 2026, updated](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-listen-labs/); [SiliconANGLE, 1 Oct 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/01/salesforce-to-acquire-ai-customer-research-startup-listen-labs-in-reported-2b-deal/)). Yet each vendor grades its own headline accuracy figure, on a metric of its choosing. The independent tests published in 2026 point the other way. Pew found an average error of **12 points** ([Pew Research Center, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really.md)). A Prolific study found that demographic personas **tripled distributional error** ([Research Live, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/synthetic-cant-substitute-measurement-finds-study/id/5152227)). The "funhouse mirrors" mega-study put the twin–human correlation at **about 0.2** ([arXiv 2509.19088, v5 19 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19088)). Synthetic respondents can approximate averages, but they compress variance, stereotype subgroups and fail at the level of the individual. **No company found builds agents from choices people make inside a designed interactive world, and none runs a consumer loop in which a person, their friends and their twin compete to predict that person.** Competitors build from what people say or from LLM priors; Aaru's transaction-trained synthetic populations are the main "do" claim. The addressable market is also narrower than the headlines suggest. ESOMAR's ~US$165bn 2025 "insights industry" figure includes research software. Market research proper was **US$56bn in 2024** ([Research World/ESOMAR, 4 Nov 2025](https://researchworld.com/articles/inside-the-153bn-insights-industry)). The online-sample business that a behavioural panel would displace is roughly **US$1–3bn** and under financial stress. Precedents show that people will generate behavioural data in games at scale: pymetrics scored 4.2M job applications ([Bommasani et al., arXiv 2605.27371, 26 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27371)). But the best published link found between in-game and real-world behaviour is about r = 0.5, measured on 49 people ([Coutrot et al., PLOS ONE, 18 Mar 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213272)), and friend-network formats peak within weeks and fade within months. Under GDPR, an echo is profiling and personal data for as long as it exists. An echo built only from behaviour avoids the AI Act's emotion-recognition and biometric provisions, but it becomes high-risk as soon as anyone uses it to hire, lend, insure or influence voting. ECHO's "do, not say" thesis aims at exactly the gap the independent studies identify. It remains a hypothesis until a pre-registered, held-out test shows echoes built from behaviour beating demographic and LLM baselines for individual people.

## Companies: billions raised, every accuracy figure self-graded

Four groups crowd around ECHO's thesis without occupying it. The first is the simulated-society and twin startups: Simile, Aaru, Artificial Societies, Electric Twin and a long tail. The second is synthetic-respondent vendors, mostly cheap and mostly built on LLM priors. The third is research incumbents turning their own panels into twins. The fourth is adjacent players: AI-moderated interview platforms, consumer "digital twin" apps and game-based profilers.

The tables below separate what each company claims from what anyone has independently checked. They also record what each one builds its agents from: **Say** (interviews, surveys, documents), **Do** (observed behaviour, transactions, outcomes) or **LLM priors** (what a base model already knows, conditioned on demographics).

**Simile is the benchmark the rest of the category is measured against.** It came out of stealth with Index's $100M. Its backers include Andrej Karpathy, Fei-Fei Li, Adam D'Angelo and Guillermo Rauch ([Simile blog, undated 2026](https://www.simile.com/blog/the-simulation-company)). Five months later Greenoaks led a round at $2B ([TechCrunch, 30 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3148353)). Its stated mission is to simulate "all eight billion people on earth". TechCrunch's reporter called that mission "preposterous", while still calling simulated users "a promising area" ([TechCrunch, 30 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3148353)).

What sets Simile apart is that each agent matches one real, consenting person. Agents are built from "structured interviews, past choices and behavioral signals", and the investor contrasts this with tools that start from demographic profiles ([BCV, 30 Jul 2026](https://baincapitalventures.com/insight/the-human-layer-of-ai-why-we-re-continuing-to-invest-in-simile/)). CVS used **400,000 agentic twins** to work out how to get patients to take their medication ([TNW, 30 Jul 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/simile-200-million-agentic-twins-ai-market-research)).

Park lists four kinds of commercial data: life-story interviews; transaction and scraped web data; lab or virtual experiments with real stakes, where purchases were actually delivered ("That's ultimately what makes it behavioral"); and randomised controlled trials, which he calls the most important.

Participants are recruited "with consent, and with incentives" through panel partners that reach "tens of millions of people globally" ([Latent Space, Aug 2026](https://latent.space/p/simile/)). Park also estimates that frontier models without Simile's data reach "20, 30 percent" on niche populations and "around 50 to 60 percent" on the general population (same source; his own figures).

The "virtual experiments" line is the closest any funded competitor comes to ECHO's thesis. Even so, the published and validated core is still interviews and surveys.

The checking of Simile's evidence is thin. Its main evidence is its founders' own paper. Gallup, a partner, is validating independently. So far it has said only that simulated distributions were "close enough to approximating human responses" to justify further work, and that it "will never present them as human responses" ([Gallup, 11 May 2026](https://news.gallup.com/opinion/methodology/709373/gallup-begins-research-simulated-responses.aspx)). One industry newsletter's verdict on the category is blunt: "Every accuracy figure is a self-graded exam" ([Trends.vc, 3 Sep 2026](https://trends.vc/synthetic-audiences-verified-humans-panel-fraud-consent-gap/)). Prices are not public; Park says "The larger the panel, the higher the price" ([TNW, 30 Jul 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/simile-200-million-agentic-twins-ai-market-research)). Nothing found shows that the people being modelled ever see or interact with their own twin.

**Aaru's rhetoric is the closest to ECHO's: "Ask people, and they answer. Simulate them, and they act."** ([aaru.com, accessed 5 Nov 2026](https://aaru.com/)). Co-founder Ned Koh's pitch to buyers is "Do not trust us. Do not trust our model" and "People lie" ([Fortune, 17 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/aaru-cofounder-ned-koh-ai-startup-sales-pitch-do-not-trust-us/)).

Its agents, however, are synthetic members of a population with demographic traits, not twins of consenting individuals. They are reportedly trained on credit-card and food-delivery data, and Koh did not disclose the architecture ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/aaru-cofounder-ned-koh-ai-startup-sales-pitch-do-not-trust-us/)). In 2024 the method was demographics, plus a news diet matched to each group, plus an LLM ([Semafor, 6 Nov 2024](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/06/2024/ai-startup-aaru-defends-using-artificial-intelligence-for-polling)).

Its public record is mixed: **2024 New York primary:** it says it called the result within 371 votes ([CO/AI summarising Semafor, 21 Sep 2024](https://getcoai.com/news/this-startup-uses-ai-agents-to-predict-election-outcomes-better-than-humans/)). **2025 New York City mayoral primary:** it reports landing within 2,000 votes ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/aaru-cofounder-ned-koh-ai-startup-sales-pitch-do-not-trust-us/)), but a secondary summary calls this a back-test ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/ai-startup-aaru-hits-1b-valuation-simulating-voters-and-consumers-to-predict-real-world-behavior)), and whether it was published before the vote is unclear. **2024 presidential election:** it forecast a Harris win. Semafor wrote that "like surveys of real people, Aaru got most of its predictions wrong" ([Semafor, 6 Nov 2024](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/06/2024/ai-startup-aaru-defends-using-artificial-intelligence-for-polling)).

The EY wealth-study example shows the say–do gap Aaru sells against: 82% of surveyed investors said they would keep their parents' wealth manager. Koh puts real retention at about 20–30%. Aaru's agents said about 40% ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/aaru-cofounder-ned-koh-ai-startup-sales-pitch-do-not-trust-us/)).

That validation was run by the alliance partner on its own study, not by an independent party. Fortune also notes that Koh offered no evidence for his claim that training on behaviour avoids inherited bias.

**Artificial Societies and Electric Twin are the UK challengers, and they are funded at a tenth of the leaders' scale.**

*Artificial Societies* started in 2025 with self-serve simulations of LLM personas reacting to social posts and ads ([Tech Funding News, 3 Sep 2025](https://techfundingnews.com/artificial-societies-raises-5m-seed-ai-simulations-human-behaviour/)). It now sells demo-only work in communications, crisis, investor relations and government affairs ([societies.ai](https://societies.ai/)). Its core product is agents influencing one another, which is the mechanic closest to ECHO's living villages. Its agents, however, are personas rather than twins of specific people. Its October 2026 "Rothko" model is trained to output decisions rather than free text. In the company's own evaluation, it had "comfortably the lowest error on every measure" against nine frontier LLMs ([MRweb, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.mrweb.com/drno/news40505.htm)).

*Electric Twin* was founded by former Number 10 officials: **What it builds:** audience models from clients' own surveys and focus groups. **Government angle:** it markets deployment in classified environments for government clients but names no government customer ([electrictwin.com](https://www.electrictwin.com/)). **Evidence:** its pre-registered benchmark uses human data collected by a third party (Stack Data Strategy), making it the most careful vendor test found. It was still designed with the company's own Chief Science Adviser. **Investor conflict:** the two press reports disagree on whether Marc Andreessen joined the round or invested as an angel ([Research Live, 12 Feb 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/electric-twin-raises-10m-in-funding-round/id/5146836); [CFOtech, 13 Feb 2026](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/electric-twin-raises-usd-14m-to-scale-ai-audiences)).

Trends.vc puts two young persona-network companies at "under $20M in funding combined" ([Trends.vc, 3 Sep 2026](https://trends.vc/synthetic-audiences-verified-humans-panel-fraud-consent-gap/)).

The long tail matters mostly as a signal of design choices: **Semilattice** is the only one with public prices, and it benchmarks directly against the academic twin literature ([semilattice.ai](https://semilattice.ai/)). **Subconscious** validates estimated choice parameters, not individuals ([subconscious.ai](https://www.subconscious.ai/)). **Altera's Project Sid** ran 10 to 1,000+ LLM agents in Minecraft, where they developed specialised roles and passed on cultural practices ([arXiv 2411.00114, 31 Oct 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)). It is the only precedent found for a society simulated inside a game world. It rests purely on LLM priors and makes no prediction claims. The company's site was unreachable on 5 November 2026, and its status is UNVERIFIED. **Helm, CulturePulse and Seldon** are named as competitors in coverage but are UNVERIFIED ([TNW, 30 Jul 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/simile-200-million-agentic-twins-ai-market-research); [TechCrunch, 5 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/ai-synthetic-research-startup-aaru-raised-a-series-a-at-a-1b-headline-valuation/); [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/synthetic-audiences-verified-humans-panel-fraud-consent-gap/)).

These vendors compete on price and speed, not on proof.

**Synthetic Users' "parity"** is the overlap of qualitative themes between synthetic and real interviews. It cannot be compared with any correlation or error metric. The company's own science post reports an "average of 15% deviation" in early 2024, measured by "manual theme extraction" ([Synthetic Users, undated](https://www.syntheticusers.com/science-posts/comparison-studies-the-opportunity-lies-in-the-deviation)).

**Expected Parrot** is research infrastructure rather than a panel. Its own repository warns that simulated responses "reflect statistical patterns, not the actual opinions of any demographic group" ([EDSL GitHub](https://github.com/expectedparrot/edsl)). That makes it a likely source of the evaluation tooling an echo would be benchmarked with.

**Ask Rally's** founder separated two goals that ECHO should also keep apart: personas that seem human and personas that predict choices. Rally's personas are calibrated "until they can trick a superhuman LLM judge in a modified Turing test" ([askrally.com](https://askrally.com/)). Yet in his own test, "every single model failed catastrophically at predicting how students and parents respond to education marketing" ([Every, 29 Aug 2025](https://every.to/also-true-for-humans/the-most-human-ai-model-is-also-one-of-the-cheapest)).

**The EY "95% correlation" claim has to be untangled, because two competing vendors both cite it.** The better-sourced trail leads to Evidenza: **The quote.** EY Americas' CMO told Adweek, "It was astounding that the matches were so similar. I mean, it was 95% correlation." She was describing a synthetic sample built to match EY's annual survey of senior executives. **Who relayed it.** The quote comes through Mark Ritson's column, and Ritson discloses a shareholding in Evidenza ([MiniMBA/Ritson, 14 Jun 2024](https://minimba.com/articles/synthetic-data-is-as-good-as-real-next-comes-synthetic-strategy)). **Evidenza's own wording.** Its site rephrases the result as "the conclusions were 95% the same", which is a different quantity ([evidenza.ai](https://www.evidenza.ai/)). **A third account.** Solomon Partners, an investment bank, describes the test without naming the vendor: a "double-blind test" of "a thousand synthetic personas" against EY's survey of CEOs, finding "a 95% correlation" ([Solomon Partners, 8 Sep 2025](http://solomonpartners.com/insights/reports/synthetic-data-is-transforming-market-research/)). **FishDog.** Its homepage repeats "EY Americas found 95% correlation" without a source ([fishdog-web.pages.dev](https://fishdog-web.pages.dev/)).

None of these sources defines the correlation (which items, at what level) or gives the sample. So the figure is a client's statement about one study, relayed by an interested party. It is not a validation. Whether EY tested Evidenza, FishDog or both remains UNVERIFIED.

EY also appears as the validating partner in Aaru's alliance ([EY, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2026/09/ey-announces-alliance-with-aaru-to-help-organizations-drive-growth-with-greater-confidence-through-behavioral-simulation)). A large client lending its name to several vendors' tests is a pattern a sober reader should notice.

The tail is drifting from cheap LLM personas toward models of individual respondents trained on real panel data, as with Fairgen, Lakmoos and Brox. Even Fairgen's CEO now writes the following in an advertisement feature ([Research Live, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/opinion/generative-ai-changed-everything-fully-synthetic-audiences-didnt/id/5148703)): fully synthetic LLM audiences "too often failed to capture the nuance and variance"; "the data underneath matters more than the model on top"; and twins are "second-best to fieldwork".

In vendor copy, "behavioural" almost always means one of three things: self-reported survey answers about behaviour, CRM or transaction data, or census and market signals. **No vendor describes building personas from people's observed choices inside an interactive environment.**

### Independent tests find twins compress variance and miss subgroups

**Park et al.'s "85%", the figure the industry quotes most, is a normalised ratio, not raw accuracy.** The original paper, published 15 November 2024, built agents from two-hour interviews with 1,052 Americans. The agents reproduced participants' General Social Survey answers "85% as accurately as participants replicate their own answers two weeks later" ([arXiv 2411.10109 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1)). In other words, the agent's match rate is divided by how consistently each person matches their own earlier answers.

The current version was revised on 28 June 2026 and retitled "LLM Agents Grounded in Self-Reports Enable General-Purpose Simulation of Individuals". On held-out GSS items it reports ([arXiv 2411.10109 v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)):

| Agent built from | Share of test–retest consistency reached |
|---|---|
| Interviews only | 83% |
| Surveys only | 82% |
| Interviews and surveys combined | 86% |
| Demographics only | 74% |

The paper itself says the "gains over either source alone were modest".

On attitude items, then, a person's own words add roughly 8–12 normalised points over a demographic sketch. Retellings often drop the normalisation. Bain, for example, wrote that agents "matched human survey responses with 85% accuracy" ([Bain, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-synthetic-customers-bring-companies-closer-to-the-real-ones/)). A competitor puts the paper's raw exact-answer match at 65.7% (competitor framing, [semilattice.ai](https://semilattice.ai/)). The Columbia team adds a further caveat: Park et al. correlate across questions within each participant, which yields higher numbers than correlating across people ([arXiv 2509.19088 v3, 7 Nov 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19088v3)).

**The largest independent test is the "funhouse mirrors" mega-study, and it is sobering.** Peng, Toubia and 21 co-authors ran 19 pre-registered studies with 164 outcomes on the Twin-2K-500 panel. In that panel, 2,058 people answered about 500 questions each ([arXiv 2505.17479, 23 May 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17479)). The paper was published in *Science Advances* on 2 September 2026 ([arXiv 2509.19088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19088)). Its findings: **Accuracy is modest.** Twin–human correlation is "approximately 0.2 on average", and twins are "only modestly more accurate than… a homogeneous base LLM". They named five distortions: insufficient individuation, stereotyping, representation bias, ideological bias and hyper-rationality. **Accuracy in a different metric.** An earlier version put average individual accuracy at 0.748. A random benchmark scores 0.629 and human test–retest 0.817. **Answers are too uniform.** Twin answers were less spread out than real people's in 154 of 164 cases (93.9%) ([arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19088v3)). **Accuracy is skewed.** Twins were more accurate for people with higher education and income (same source). **Twins barely beat demographics.** Science News summarised that twins were "wrong on average about a quarter of the time" and performed "roughly on par with chatbots that received demographic information alone" ([Science News, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/ai-agents-replace-humans-research)).

**Two panel firms ran the most cited 2026 tests, and both concluded "not a substitute".**

*Pew* built "digital twins" of members of its American Trends Panel from their demographics and political-typology answers. It replicated nearly 300 questions across three 2026 survey waves, using the best model it tested ([Pew, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really.md)): **Overall error:** 12.4 points on average. Error exceeded 15 points on about 28% of questions. **Subgroup error:** up to 16.1 points for Republicans and 15.1 for Black adults. **Stereotyping:** 97% of synthetic Hispanic adults "follow the World Cup", against 43% of real ones. **Knowledge inflation:** 98% answered a First Amendment question correctly, against 52% of real people. **Missing uncertainty and range:** real people chose "not sure" about four times as often. Nearly half of the questions had an answer option that no synthetic respondent chose.

Pew concludes "we see no substitute for rigorous, multimode, probability-based surveys".

*Prolific* studied 996 politically representative US participants ([Research Live, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/synthetic-cant-substitute-measurement-finds-study/id/5152227)): Giving the model demographic personas "roughly tripled the distributional error" compared with asking it for an aggregate distribution. The personas collapsed onto stereotyped modal answers. Adding interview transcripts halved that collapse but still did not beat the no-persona baseline. "No method matched the accuracy of a second human sample." Judging methods on a single metric "rewards exactly the wrong methods".

Both firms sell human data, as does Verasight, whose 2025 test found topline errors of 4–23 points ([Verasight, 18 Aug 2025](https://www.verasight.io/reports/synthetic-sampling)). Their conflicts of interest are real. Even so, their findings match the academic record: Bisbee et al. found lower variance, regression coefficients that differ from real data, and results that drift "over a 3-month period" ([Political Analysis, 2024](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1017/pan.2024.5)). Wang, Morgenstern and Dickerson found that LLMs "flatten identity groups" ([arXiv 2402.01908; *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01908)). Jia et al. built personas from Dutch panel histories. Aggregate alignment improved but individual-level prediction stayed weak ([arXiv 2605.10659, accepted NeurIPS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10659)). Tigre and Souto show that close aggregate agreement can hide subgroup error. Nominal 95% intervals covered only 88–94% of cases in some regimes ([arXiv 2609.13148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13148)).

The positive results are narrower than vendors imply: **Argyle et al.** coined "algorithmic fidelity" for subgroup distributions ([arXiv 2209.06899, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06899)). **Maier et al.** reached "90% of human test-retest reliability" on purchase intent. This was an industry-authored paper using a special elicitation method across 57 product surveys ([arXiv 2510.08338, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08338)). **Bain** reports a client backtest in which digital twins "replicated about 90% of key outcomes" ([Bain, 4 May 2026](https://www.bain.com/insights/synthetic-customers-earn-their-stripes/)). **Centaur** is the strongest result for ECHO's thesis. It is a language model fine-tuned on more than 10 million trial-level *choices* from more than 60,000 lab participants. It predicted held-out participants better than existing cognitive models and generalised to new domains ([Binz et al., *Nature*, 2 Jul 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4)). Its data came from lab tasks, not consumer markets.

**The say–do gap gives ECHO's thesis its theoretical footing, and it also shows how most current validation is circular.** Three meta-analyses quantify the gap: A medium-to-large change in intention (d = 0.66) produces only a small-to-medium change in behaviour (d = 0.36) ([Webb & Sheeran, *Psychological Bulletin*, 2006](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249)). Hypothetical willingness to pay exceeds real willingness to pay by 21% on average. Indirect methods such as conjoint overstate it *more* than direct questions do ([Schmidt & Bijmolt, *JAMS*, 2020](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6?fields=title,year,venue,abstract)). In stated-preference valuation, the median ratio of hypothetical to actual value is 1.35 ([Murphy et al., 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/enreec/v30y2005i3p313-325.html)).

The circularity follows directly. A synthetic respondent "validated" against human conjoint or survey answers may simply reproduce hypothetical bias rather than predict purchases.

LLM twins also err in two opposite directions. Some come out overly positive ("overly positive and agreeable", [Bain, 2025](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-synthetic-customers-bring-companies-closer-to-the-real-ones/)). Others come out hyper-rational, and Qualtrics reported synthetic humans "far less susceptible to priming than real humans" ([Forrester, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/power-growth-with-synthetic-data-lessons-from-qualtrics-x4/)). Either way they diverge from what people actually do.

**What each group builds its agents from:**

| Basis | Who |
|---|---|
| **Say** (interviews, surveys, documents) | Simile's validated core, Electric Twin, Semilattice, Fairgen, the incumbents' panel twins, every interview platform, and most consumer twins |
| **Do** (claims of training on behaviour, outcomes, transactions or product analytics) | Aaru, Artificial Societies' Rothko, NIQ's purchase panels, Haven, and the YC startups Studio and Promi (see the YC section) |
| **LLM priors** | Ask Rally, Synthetic Users, Evidenza, Yabble, Subconscious, Expected Parrot's tooling, Project Sid |

**No one builds agents from choices that identifiable, consenting people make in a designed interactive environment, and no one validates against those same people's later choices.** That empty slot is ECHO's position. It is empty partly because nobody has yet shown it is worth filling.

The defensible market for an echo product rests on substitution, not on the US$165bn headline: **Research services:** some share of the ~US$35bn of full-service research and fieldwork inside ESOMAR's 2024 market-research sector. **Panels:** some share of the roughly US$1–3bn spent on survey sample.

No reputable source sizes "synthetic respondents" or "AI in market research". No current source gives the share of marketing budgets spent on research. ESOMAR and the IA both measure supplier revenue, so in-house insights teams are invisible in both. Since marketing budgets are flat and AI already takes about 15% of them, an echo product would most likely compete for the research-software and AI-tooling line, not for new money.

**The panel business that a behavioural panel would replace is shrinking and financially stressed.** The fast-growing panel business is selling human data to AI labs, not market research.

**Cint.** Net sales fell 9.5% to EUR 150.4m in 2025, after a EUR 193.2m impairment on its Lucid acquisition. The CEO said "research budgets continued to lag 2024 levels" ([Cint year-end report, 19 Feb 2026](https://www.mfn.se/a/cint-group/cint-group-year-end-report-2025)). A Triton-backed bidder then took Cint private at SEK 5.60 a share. It passed 93% ownership on 16 July 2026, and Cint applied for delisting ([Cint via Inderes, Jul 2026](https://www.inderes.fi/en/releases/cint-has-applied-for-delisting-of-its-shares-from-nasdaq-stockholm)). **Dynata.** It went through a prepackaged Chapter 11 in May 2024 that cut its debt from $1.3bn to $780m ([Research Live, 23 May 2024](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/dynata_applies_for_chapter_11_in_us_bankruptcy_court/id/5126273)). Law360 gave the debt as $1.4bn (search-excerpt-only). On 30 July 2026 it launched a distressed debt exchange ([Bloomberg Law, Jul 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/dynata-launches-distressed-exchange-two-years-after-bankruptcy)). **YouGov.** Revenue grew 2% to £396.6m in FY26, and adjusted operating profit fell 11%. Management says "human panel data remains central" ([Quartr AI-generated summary, Oct 2026](https://quartr.com/events/yougov-plc-you-h2-2026_Fka0kak3)). **Qualtrics.** It went the other way, agreeing to buy Press Ganey Forsta for $6.75bn ([Research Live, 7 Oct 2025](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/qualtrics-to-acquire-press-ganey-forsta/id/5143493)). **Prolific** is the growth exception. It raised a £25m Series A in July 2023 and has been profitable since 2020 ([Prolific, 12 Jul 2023](https://www.prolific.com/blog/series-a-fundraise)). It reached roughly US$350M of annualised revenue by April 2026, gross of participant pay, driven by AI labs rather than market research (analyst estimate, [Sacra, 19 May 2026](https://sacra.com/research/350m-year-mercor-for-human-personality/)).

**Data quality is the strongest argument both for and against synthetic respondents.** The evidence on contamination of human panels: **Bogus respondents.** In Pew's 2024 study, 12% of opt-in respondents under 30 claimed to be licensed to operate a nuclear submarine ([Pew, 5 Mar 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/03/05/online-opt-in-polls-can-produce-misleading-results-especially-for-young-people-and-hispanic-adults/)). **LLM-written answers.** An estimated 33–46% of crowd workers used LLMs on a 2023 summarisation task ([arXiv 2306.07899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07899)). **AI agents that pass checks.** An autonomous agent passed 99.8% of 6,000 attention checks at about 5 cents a response, against about $1.50 paid to humans. As few as 10–52 fake responses would have flipped major pre-election polls ([404 Media on Westwood, PNAS, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.404media.co/a-researcher-made-an-ai-that-completely-breaks-the-online-surveys-scientists-rely-on/)). Prolific, which has a commercial interest, replied that its own check told humans from agents with 100% accuracy ([Research Live, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/opinion/the-right-safeguards-can-protect-public-opinion-polls-from-ai-pollution/id/5148723)). **Criminal fraud.** Eight defendants were indicted over $10m of fabricated survey data sold through Op4G and Slice ([Research Live, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/eight-charged-with-survey-fraud-in-us/id/5138203)). The fabricated data reached NIH-funded studies, and one paper has been retracted ([Retraction Watch, 6 Aug 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/08/06/op4g-market-research-indictment-fake-data/)). **Buyer confidence.** Only 33% of 173 client-side researchers were confident that poor-quality respondents are removed (convenience sample, [Research World, 28 Sep 2026](https://researchworld.com/articles/what-do-clients-think-and-do-about-quality)).

The industry's response to contamination, however, is "verified human" provenance. The Insights Association's CEO says decisions should be "grounded in trusted, verified, human understanding" ([Insights Association, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.insightsassociation.org/News/Industry-News/ArticleID/2650/U-S-Insights-Analytics-Industry-Reaches-89-3-Billion-as-AI-and-Technology-Reshape-the-Market)). LLM-only respondents are exactly what fraud detection is built to exclude.

An echo grounded in consented, identifiable people's observed choices sits between the two positions. It is synthetic output with human provenance, and it would have to document that provenance to be bought.

### Analysts and industry bodies accept synthetic customers only when they are grounded, disclosed and backtested

**Gartner has moved from "synthetic data fixes supply and privacy" to "synthetic customers let you test decisions".** **2022:** it said synthetic data "will completely overshadow real data in AI models" by 2030 (search-excerpt-only, [Gartner, 22 Jun 2022](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-06-22-is-synthetic-data-the-future-of-ai)). **2024 forecast:** "By 2026, 75% of businesses will use generative AI to create synthetic customer data, up from less than 5% in 2023" ([SAS reproduction of Gartner Predicts 2024, 28 Feb 2024](https://www.sas.com/content/dam/sasdam/documents/20250124/why-synthetic-data-is-essential-for-your-organizations-ai-driven-future.pdf)). This was a prediction, not a measurement. A blog's claim that 75% of firms *already* use synthetic data is UNVERIFIED. **Definition of a "digital twin of the customer":** a "dynamic virtual mirror representation of a customer that simulates and learns to emulate and anticipate behavior". The same 2023 survey found only 27% of supply-chain chiefs planning one (search-excerpt-only, [Gartner, 20 Jul 2023](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2023-07-20-gartner-survey-shows-just-27-percent-of-chief-supply-chain-officers-plan-to-implement-a-digital-twin-of-the-customer)). The definition is about behaviour rather than stated attitudes, which places it closer to ECHO than to LLM survey personas. **2026 research notes:** "Synthetic Customers Are the Next Frontier for VoC Innovation" ([Gartner, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7470026)) and "Use AI Synthetic Customers to Test Decisions Before You Make Them" ([Gartner, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/8280421)). Both are visible only as paywalled abstracts. **Vendor race note:** "AI Vendor Race: Startups to Watch in Synthetic Population and Behavioral Simulation" (14 Apr 2026). It is known publicly only through one named vendor's announcement ([MRS, Verve news, 13 May 2026](https://www.mrs.org.uk/researchcompany-news/verve/news/5977)). **Hype Cycle:** no placement for synthetic respondents could be verified.

**Bain is the most outspoken consultancy, and it attaches conditions.** **June 2025:** synthetic customers layered on real research "can deliver comparable insights in half the time and at one-third the cost". The same piece warns that bots "can be overly positive and agreeable" and are "not a plug-and-play replacement" ([Bain, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-synthetic-customers-bring-companies-closer-to-the-real-ones/)). **May 2026:** a client backtest "replicated about 90% of key outcomes". Bain also concluded "No off-the-shelf solution currently meets all requirements" and told firms to "Backtest to prove reliability", to rely on first-party rather than vendor data, and to "start by augmenting" ([Bain, 4 May 2026](https://www.bain.com/insights/synthetic-customers-earn-their-stripes/)).

**Forrester** is cautiously enthusiastic. Its analyst Dipanjan Chatterjee called synthetic data "a game-changer" while asking "do fake people learn biases from real people?" and concluding that "the truth is likely in between" ([Forrester, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/power-growth-with-synthetic-data-lessons-from-qualtrics-x4/)).

**IDC, McKinsey and BCG:** no public statement on synthetic respondents was found. Each was searched at most once, so this is not conclusive.

**The binding professional standard is now the 2025 ICC/ESOMAR International Code,** approved on 25 June 2025 ([MRWeb, 25 Jun 2025](https://www.mrweb.com/drno/news38456.htm)). It defines a "synthetic persona" as "a digital representation of a person generated to mimic the behaviours, preferences, and characteristics of real people or groups" ([ICC/ESOMAR Code 2025](https://community.esomar.org/uploads/public/knowledge-and-standards/codes-and-guidelines/ICCESOMAR-International-Code_English.pdf)). It imposes three disclosure duties: Clients must be told when synthetic data or personas are used, and "the extent of human oversight must be stated" (Art. 7(e)). Published findings must disclose whether AI or synthetic data "played a significant role" (Art. 9(b)). People must be told at the start when a synthetic persona collects data from them (Art. 4(a)(ii)).

ESOMAR's own leadership sums it up: "Disclosure is mandatory" ([Research World, 8 Jan 2026](https://researchworld.com/articles/why-the-icc-esomar-code-will-matter-more-than-ever-in-2026)).

**The UK's MRS** has been consistent: Its 2024 Delphi report says synthetic data "could be a valuable addition" but warns of "normative results which can flatten diverse groups" ([Research Live, 16 May 2024](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/mrs-delphi-group-releases-synthetic-data-report/id/5126013)). Its 2026 explainer separates synthetic personas from digital twins, defining a digital twin as one simulated individual. It calls personas "not a replacement for human research" ([Research Live, 13 May 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/featuress/terms-of-engagement-synthetic-personas/id/5149273)).

**The US Insights Association** reportedly advises disclosing synthetic participants and never presenting modelled outputs as observed behaviour (search-excerpt-only, [Insights Association](https://www.insightsassociation.org/News-Updates/Articles/ArticleID/2614/Code-in-Action-Synthetic-Data-Revisited)).

**Adoption surveys diverge according to who runs them.**

*Qualtrics,* which sells synthetic responses: **2025 Market Research Trends report (released October 2024):** 69% of its 3,000+ researchers said they had used synthetic responses in the past year. 71% expect "the 'majority of market research'" to be synthetic within three years ([Research Live, 24 Oct 2024](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/researchers-predict-synthetic-responses-will-dominate-in-coming-years/id/5131923)). **2026 report (released November 2025):** among adopters, 45% call synthetic data their most reliable source ([Research Live, 19 Nov 2025](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/most-researchers-using-ai-regularly-finds-qualtrics-study/id/5144776)). **Conflicting figure:** a Qualtrics executive later cited 62% use over six months ([Greenbook, 6 Jun 2025](https://www.greenbook.org/insights/grit/smarter-insights-faster-pace-ais-breakthrough-in-market-research)).

*Greenbook's GRIT 2026,* a practitioner survey (n = 735, fieldwork Q1 2026), is the better read on actual practice: Use among brand-side research teams rose from **8% to 26%** in one wave. Use reached 57% in brand analytics teams and 44% among suppliers with more than 500 staff. Part of the jump comes from widening the survey label to include AI personas and digital twins. GRIT describes "active, sometimes anxious evaluation" ([Greenbook GRIT 2026](https://www.greenbook.org/grit/insights-practice-edition/ai-readable)).

*GRIT's 2025 business survey* adds the budget context: only 34% of research buyers cite management support for budget increases, down from 65% ([Greenbook, 18 Jun 2025](https://greenbook.org/insights/artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning/the-sea-change-insights-from-the-2025-grit-business-outlook)).

**The large research firms all draw the same line.** **Kantar** tested GPT-4 against about 5,000 real respondents. It found the model "overly positive" and "not sensitive enough" for subgroup analysis, and concluded that off-the-shelf synthetic sample is "just not good enough to use as a supplement for human sample" ([Kantar, 5 Jul 2023](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Analytics/What-is-synthetic-sample-and-is-it-all-its-cracked-up-to-be)). **Ipsos** calls persona bots "risky business", warns buyers against "snake oil salesmen", and tells them to ask "How did you validate your synthetic data models?" ([Ipsos Views, Jun 2025](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2025-08/Personas%20In%20The%20Age%20Of%20AI.pdf)). **NIQ** says "today's GenAI models alone are insufficient" and limits synthetic respondents to "early-stage development, when the stakes are lower" ([NIQ, 10 Jun 2024](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/commentary/2024/it-takes-one-to-know-one-confronting-the-duality-of-synthetic-respondents-in-market-research/)).

**The consensus, then:** Ground synthetic respondents in proprietary human data, preferably behavioural. Backtest them. Disclose their use. Keep them to directional or screening work.

None of these bodies or firms calls synthetic respondents decision-grade for a go/no-go call without human confirmation. That consensus favours ECHO's *grounding* but hands the validation burden to whoever sells it.

## Game-based assessment: millions played, but validity was never published

**pymetrics showed that people will produce behavioural data through games at scale. It also showed that a category leader can scale for years without publishing evidence that game behaviour predicts anything.**

*Funding and adoption.* Frida Polli founded pymetrics in 2013. It raised a $40M Series B led by General Atlantic in September 2018, joined by Salesforce Ventures and Workday Ventures. Total funding is variously given as $58M, about $57M and $59.5M ([VentureBeat, 27 Sep 2018](https://venturebeat.com/business/pymetrics-raises-40-million-for-ai-powered-job-recruitment); [Global Venturing, search-excerpt-only](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/pymetrics-picks-out-corporates-to-raise-40m/); [StartupIntros, aggregator-only](https://startupintros.com/news/2018-09-01-pymetrics-series-b)). By 2018, more than a million candidates had played its games, for customers including Unilever, Hyatt and Accenture ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/pymetrics-raises-40-million-for-ai-powered-job-recruitment)).

*The games.* There were twelve games "derived from peer-reviewed psychological studies", and they were "not meant to be won or lost" ([Wilson et al., FAccT 2021](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amislove/publications/Pymetrics-FAccT.pdf)). They measured traits such as risk-taking, trust, altruism and planning.

*How scoring worked.* For each client, a classifier treated current employees as positive examples. Between December 2018 and December 2022 it scored **4,197,168 applications from 3,372,132 applicants** at 156 employers ([Bommasani et al., arXiv 2605.27371, 26 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27371)).

*Fairness, not validity, was what got audited.* The Northeastern team's fairness audit (FAccT 2021) agreed in advance not to test validity. Its scope excluded "the ability of pymetrics' games to measure human capabilities, whether those capabilities map to job performance, or whether other assessment methods would be superior". The auditors saw only a confidential validation presentation and urged pymetrics to "make these results public" ([Wilson et al., FAccT 2021](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amislove/publications/Pymetrics-FAccT.pdf)). The 2026 Stanford study likewise "does not measure the validity of the gameplay features". It did find that 4% of applicants who applied to ten positions were recommended for rejection from all of them ([Bommasani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27371)).

*What happened to it.* Harver completed its acquisition of pymetrics on 11 August 2022, on undisclosed terms ([Harver, 11 Aug 2022](https://harver.com/?p=36917)). By July 2025 the games were being audited as "Harver's Soft Skills Platform". The 2025 bias audit lists 119,242 women and 164,014 men (283,256 applicants with a known gender, calc). Gender was unknown for 191,455 applicants and race for 317,513 ([BABL AI audit summary, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.sc.com/en/uploads/sites/66/content/docs/Harver_SSP_Public-Summary-2.pdf)).

*Two lessons for ECHO.*
**Game play carries demographic signal.** The FAccT auditors found that the distribution of gameplay features differed significantly by gender (29.7–46.9% of features) and by race (17.5–33.4%). An echo built from choices will therefore encode protected attributes even if ECHO never collects them. **The category became a feature of HR suites, not a standalone market.** HireVue bought MindX in 2018 ([PR Newswire, 10 May 2018](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/hirevue-acquires-mindx-to-create-hr-industrys-most-robust-ai-based-talent-assessments-suite-300646062.html)), Criteria bought Revelian in 2020 ([Criteria, 3 Mar 2020](https://www.criteriacorp.com/press-releases/2020/criteria-corp-backed-by-sumeru-equity-partners-acquires-revelian-the-leader)), and Harver bought pymetrics in 2022. All three deals had undisclosed terms.

**Arctic Shores** grew from more than 500,000 candidates assessed in 2019 to more than three million by 2023: **Funding:** a £4.52M Series A ([UK Tech News, 2 Sep 2019](https://www.uktechnews.info/?p=1074)) and a £5.75M Series B ([BusinessCloud, 16 Jan 2023](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/psychometric-test-firm-raises-5-75m-in-series-b-funding/); [Just Entrepreneurs, Jan 2023](https://justentrepreneurs.co.uk/news/arctic-shores-closes-575m-series-b-to-spearhead-the-soft-skills-assessment-market-and-accelerate-growth)). **Customers:** PwC, Siemens, Airbus and Thales. **Evidence:** every outcome figure is company-reported. **Positioning:** its site now says "task-based" rather than "game-based". It markets itself as "future-proofed against ChatGPT", which is unverified marketing ([arcticshores.com, accessed 5 Nov 2026](https://www.arcticshores.com/)). The shift suggests that the "game" label became a liability with HR buyers, while measurement based on behaviour gained value once generative AI could complete text tests.

**Knack**'s Wasabi Waiter, its Shell study, its funding and its fate are all UNVERIFIED.

**The independent evidence supports "games measure about as well as tests", not "games reveal people better".** A 2022 systematic review of 34 articles concluded that game-related assessments "can be used in personnel selection". It also found: Their advantages over conventional tests are "fewer than imagined". "We cannot consider that GRA has greater predictive validity than other methods." Applicants react to them only slightly more positively than to tests. Claims of less faking and less adverse impact "still lack scientific support". Construct validity is "inconclusive". Games designed to measure Big Five facets measured cognitive ability instead, and game trace data predicted cognitive scores but not conscientiousness ([Ramos-Villagrasa et al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 28 Sep 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.952002/full)).

That last finding is the most important one for an echo. **Choices made in a game may reflect skill and attention as much as values.**

These projects establish volume beyond doubt. They also mark the limits of what game data can show.

**Embedding the task in a game people already enjoy produces the largest volumes.** Borderlands Science and EVE, both mini-games inside commercial games, dwarf standalone science games. That supports ECHO's choice to collect choices inside a world people want to be in.

**Most of these projects harvest skill or perception labour rather than personal traits.** Only Sea Hero Quest, the Moral Machine and the Great Brain Experiment treat a player's behaviour as data about that player.

**Even the best game-to-real-world link is moderate,** at r ≈ 0.5 on a perceptual-motor skill. In that same study, path integration did not correlate with real-world performance, and the sex gap was larger in the game (d = 0.89) than on the street (d = 0.59) ([PLOS ONE, 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213272)).

**The format of a choice can manufacture the preference it records.** The Moral Machine's authors concede: "Actual consumer choices cannot yet be recorded… we can only collect stated preferences, based on hypothetical choices" ([Awad et al., Nature, 4 Mar 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e57f82eb306fc38c7637f33/t/5ed51626710a624d85580d53/1616616961787/life-and-death-decisions.pdf)). Their critics showed how much the format matters. Forced to choose, 87.7% of respondents chose to save women. Given an equality option, 97.9% chose to treat both groups equally ([Bigman & Gray, Nature, 4 Mar 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e57f82eb306fc38c7637f33/t/5ed51626710a624d85580d53/1616616961787/life-and-death-decisions.pdf)).

**The cleanest published test of "do versus say" cuts both ways.** For 585,531 users, the Moral Machine authors compared in-game choices with the preferences users stated on sliders. The two agreed on eight of nine dimensions. On social status they diverged: choices favoured high-status characters, while users said status should not count ([Awad et al., 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e57f82eb306fc38c7637f33/t/5ed51626710a624d85580d53/1616616961787/life-and-death-decisions.pdf)). Behavioural choices therefore add the most information where social desirability suppresses honest self-report. They are not uniformly more revealing.

**Incentives distort data.** CCP had to tune EVE's rewards so they "aren't so great that players fudge the data" ([Vice, 23 Jun 2016](https://www.vice.com/en/article/eve-online-project-discovery)). About 10% of players did worse than always guessing the most common class ([Nature Biotechnology, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.4225)).

**Effects shrink outside the lab.** The stop-signal effect was d = 0.56 in the Great Brain Experiment app against 1.81 in the lab ([PLOS ONE, 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0100662)).

**Engagement concentrates in a small core.** Only "nearly 29,000" of more than 1.9M Project Discovery participants took part in all three phases ([EVE Online, 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/the-first-decade-of-project-discovery)).

**Overclaiming gets punished.** The Quantum Moves *Nature* paper, which claimed that players beat algorithms, was retracted in 2020 ([Wikipedia, accessed 5 Nov 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Moves)).

**Headline exchange rates should be treated as marketing.** Sea Hero Quest's "two minutes of play equals five hours of lab research" cannot be reconciled with the charity's own later figure of "nearly 2,000 hours" for four million players ([Games for Change](https://www.gamesforchange.org/games/sea-hero-quest/); [EurekAlert, 2022](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/947840)).

**Coerced play and voluntary play are different engines.** Hiring games got millions of players because play was the gate to a job. ECHO depends on voluntary play.

The compliment-only poll apps (tbh, Gas) faded but drew no regulatory action found. Platform dependence was a single point of failure. Snap's suspension effectively ended YOLO and LMK. ### Research says no single judge knows you best

The academic record supports a three-way contest with no automatic winner. That makes ECHO's question genuinely open, and it is why the game could be interesting.
**Models beat most friends.** Computer judgments from Facebook Likes correlated with people's self-rated personality at r = 0.56. Friends reached r = 0.49 and spouses r = 0.58, and the computer–spouse difference was not significant. About 10 Likes were enough to beat a colleague, 70 a friend, 150 a family member and 300 a spouse. The computer also out-predicted people's *own* self-ratings on 4 of 13 life outcomes ([Youyou, Kosinski & Stillwell, PNAS, Jan 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313801)). The human judges answered only a 10-item questionnaire. **The self knows its inner states; friends see the rest.** People judge their own internal traits, such as anxiety, better than others do. Friends judge intellect-related traits better than the self does. "On average, the people who know you best know you as well as you know yourself, no better, no worse" ([Vazire, via WashU, 2 Mar 2010](https://source.washu.edu/2010/03/others-may-know-us-better-than-we-know-ourselves-study-finds/)). Close others match the self at predicting observed daily behaviour (search-excerpt-only, [Vazire & Mehl, 2008](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/knowing-me-knowing-you-the-accuracy-and-unique-predictive-validit/)). **Familiarity can hurt.** Familiarity can reduce accuracy at predicting a partner's product preferences ([Lerouge & Warlop, JCR, 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v33y2006i3p393-402.html)). Longer relationships go with lower accuracy and more overconfidence (search-excerpt-only, [Scheibehenne, Mata & Todd, 2011](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/older-but-not-wiser-predicting-a-partner-s-preferences-gets-worse-with-age-scheibehenne-benjamin/10009127085)). **Others can predict our experiences better than we can.** People forecast their own enjoyment better from a stranger's reaction than from information about the event (search-excerpt-only, [Gilbert et al., Science, 2009](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/3110937)).

Two design consequences follow: **The yardstick.** "Your echo" should be measured against the person's own consistency, the Park et al. convention. A person guessing their own past choice will not score 100%, which makes "your echo beats you" a measurable claim rather than a slogan. **The ground truth.** Most accuracy studies score against self-report, which structurally favours the self. Scoring against choices the person actually makes later is closer to Vazire and Mehl's behaviour-based method, and fairer to friends and echo alike.

No verified 2025–2026 study pits an LLM directly against friends at predicting a specific person's choices.

## Regulation: under GDPR an echo is profiling, and how it is used sets its AI Act risk

*This section summarises the notes' reading of the law as of 5–6 November 2026. The passages marked as interpretation are inferences, not legal advice.*

### GDPR

**An echo of a specific person counts as profiling, and the echo itself is personal data for as long as it exists.** **Profiling.** GDPR Art. 4(4) defines profiling as automated processing used "to analyse or predict aspects concerning that natural person's… personal preferences, interests, reliability, behaviour". An echo built from a player's choices does exactly that ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [WP29, WP251rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/document.cfm?action=display&doc_id=49826)). **No anonymity.** The EDPB treats a trained model as anonymous only if it is "very unlikely" that individuals can be identified or their data extracted ([EDPB, Opinion 28/2024, 18 Dec 2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2024/edpb-opinion-ai-models-gdpr-principles-support-responsible-ai_en)). A per-person echo exists to reproduce one individual, so it cannot meet that test. Access, objection and erasure rights therefore apply to it throughout its life.

**Lawful basis.** The CJEU's *Meta v Bundeskartellamt* ruling limits contract necessity to processing that is "objectively indispensable". It also held that users "cannot reasonably expect" profiling for personalised advertising without consent ([CJEU C‑252/21, 4 Jul 2023](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0252)). WP251 calls intrusive profiling for marketing "difficult" to justify on legitimate interests.

*Interpretation:* the two uses are two purposes and need two lawful bases.
**Consumer loop:** contract necessity is arguable at best, so consent is the lower-risk choice. **Research use:** this needs its own unbundled consent. The request must be clearly distinguishable from other terms (Art. 7(2)), playing must not depend on it (Art. 7(4)), and withdrawal must be "as easy… as to give consent" (Art. 7(3)). **Browser telemetry:** a browser game also needs ePrivacy Art. 5(3) consent for any tracking that is not strictly necessary.

**Behaviour-only data does not escape Article 9.** The CJEU reads "revealing" special categories to include inference "following an intellectual operation involving deduction or cross-referencing" ([CJEU C‑184/20, 1 Aug 2022](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0184)). The prohibition applies whether or not the inference is accurate, and whatever the controller intends ([C‑252/21](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0252)).

*Interpretation:* moral-dilemma or "values" axes are the obvious risk, because they can reveal political or religious beliefs. ECHO would need either explicit consent for them or axes designed to avoid sensitive inference. It should never show friends a prediction on a sensitive axis. No court has yet ruled on probabilistic inference by a model as such, so this remains contested.

**Automated decisions (Art. 22).** Under *SCHUFA*, a score counts as an automated decision when a third party "draws strongly" on it to grant or end a contract ([CJEU C‑634/21, 7 Dec 2023](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0634)). Under *Dun & Bradstreet*, the person can demand "the procedure and principles actually applied", and a counterfactual explanation is offered as one acceptable form ([CJEU C‑203/22, 27 Feb 2025](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0203)).

*Interpretation:*
**Where Art. 22 does not apply:** a friends' guessing game and aggregate simulation involve no decision with legal or similar effects about the person. **Where it does:** selling per-person echo scores that clients use for hiring, credit, insurance or pricing would trigger it. **Explanations:** a game can offer counterfactual explanations naturally ("had you chosen X, your echo would predict Y").

**Impact assessment, children and erasure.** **Impact assessment (DPIA).** WP248's first high-risk criterion is "evaluation or scoring, including profiling and predicting". Its example is "a company building behavioural or marketing profiles based on usage or navigation on its website". "Innovative use" is another criterion, and meeting two criteria normally triggers an impact assessment ([WP29, WP248rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/document.cfm?action=display&doc_id=47711)). *Interpretation:* ECHO effectively needs one before launch. **Children.** Consent ages run from 13 to 16 depending on the member state ([Commission SWD(2020) 115](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52020SC0115)). WP251 advises controllers to "refrain from profiling" children for marketing and uses online gaming as its example ([WP251rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/document.cfm?action=display&doc_id=49826)). *Interpretation:* including minors' echoes in a research product is the highest-risk configuration. **Erasure.** *Interpretation:* withdrawal must delete the echo itself, including its inputs, derived parameters and cached predictions. Shared models must either be shown to be anonymous or be retrainable. **Pending reform.** The GDPR "Digital Omnibus" (COM(2025) 837) is still pending. The Council's 21 October 2026 mandate keeps the current definition of personal data. Parliament's committee vote is expected in early 2027, and the EDPB and EDPS insist that Art. 22's "general prohibition… must stay" ([nicfab, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-council/)).

### EU AI Act

**A behaviour-only echo is an AI system, and it largely avoids the Act's emotion and biometric traps.** Under the AI Act, "biometric data" covers "behavioural characteristics", with no requirement that the data identify someone. "Emotion recognition" means inferring emotions or intentions *from biometric data* ([AI Act Art. 3, reader's text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/); [Reg. (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)).

*Interpretation:* discrete choices among options are not biometric data. The caveat is fine-grained telemetry. If ECHO captured cursor or keystroke dynamics, movement kinematics or reaction-time signatures and used them to infer emotions, the system could approach emotion recognition. That would trigger a notice duty, high-risk status and prohibition in workplaces and schools. This boundary has not yet been tested in court.

**The prohibitions** have applied since 2 February 2025 ([AI Act Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). The ones relevant to ECHO cover: manipulative techniques that cause significant harm (5(1)(a)); exploiting vulnerabilities due to age (5(1)(b)); social scoring by private as well as public actors (5(1)(c)); emotion recognition at work and in education (5(1)(f)); and biometric categorisation by sensitive traits (5(1)(g)).

Secondary summaries of the Commission's non-binding February 2025 guidelines describe the social-scoring concern as "combining data from one context and using it in another to punish or exclude" (search-excerpt-only, [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/key-takeaways-from-the-european-commission-s-guide); [Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-prohibited-artificial-intelligence-ai-practices-defined-ai-act)).

**Downstream use decides the risk class.** Art. 6(3) states that an Annex III system "shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons" ([AI Act Art. 6](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/)). The Annex III uses relevant to echoes are recruitment and selection, education, credit, life and health insurance, and influencing voting behaviour ([Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)). *Interpretation:* the simplest market position is to forbid these uses in client contracts and enforce the ban.

**Transparency (Art. 50) has applied since 2 August 2026.** Echoes that converse with or act toward real people must be identifiable as AI unless that is obvious. Generated text must be machine-marked. Systems already on the market have until 2 December 2026 to comply ([nicfab, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-official-journal/)).

**On the Digital Omnibus on AI, the two notes conflict, and the regulation note is better-sourced.** *What the regulation note reports.* It cites a secondary source written after publication. That source reports the Omnibus adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**: dated 8 July 2026, published in the Official Journal on 24 July and in force on 27 July 2026. It gives the ELI as data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj ([nicfab, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-official-journal/)). A White & Case alert titled "EU AI Omnibus enters force" agrees (search-excerpt-only, [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)). *What the game-precedents note reports.* Its source was published before the Official Journal printed the regulation, so it could confirm only the earlier steps. Parliament adopted the text on 16 June 2026 and the Council gave final approval on 29 June 2026, with publication "expected in July 2026" ([SecurePrivacy, 9 Jul 2026](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-the-new-high-risk-ai-deadlines-after-council-approval)). *Why they do not truly contradict.* The two accounts describe successive steps. *Caveats.* Neither note read the Official Journal text, because EUR-Lex blocked fetching, so the adoption rests on secondary sources. The date of the political agreement also conflicts: 6 May 2026 per Gibson Dunn, 7 May per other summaries ([Gibson Dunn, 27 May 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

On that reading, the Omnibus: moves Annex III high-risk obligations to **2 December 2027** and Annex I obligations to 2 August 2028; leaves the Art. 5 prohibitions and Art. 50 untouched; adds bans on non-consensual intimate depictions of identifiable people from 2 December 2026; and allows special-category data to be used for bias detection only together with GDPR Art. 9(2)(g).

### DSA, UK and US

**The Digital Services Act's advertising and minors rules mostly wait until ECHO grows.** Arts. 26 and 28 bind online platforms. Art. 19 switches them off for micro and small enterprises (fewer than 50 staff and turnover of no more than €10m) ([DSA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)). Once ECHO grows past that, two bans apply: no ads based on profiling with special-category data, and no profiling-based ads to users known to be minors. The Commission's July 2025 guidelines on minors still work as a design benchmark: private-by-default accounts, and no streaks or notification pressure ([Commission, 14 Jul 2025](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)).

**In the UK**, the rules on automated decisions changed on 5 February 2026.
**Automated decisions.** Section 80 of the Data (Use and Access) Act replaced UK GDPR Art. 22 with Arts. 22A–22D ([SI 2026/82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/regulation/2/made)). Solely automated significant decisions are now generally allowed with safeguards, but stay restricted where special-category data are involved (search-excerpt-only, [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/automated-decision-making-duaa-uk-regime/)). **ICO guidance.** The ICO's guidance on automated decisions was still in draft after consultation closed on 29 May 2026 (search-excerpt-only). **Children.** The Children's code requires options that use profiling to be switched "off" by default. Settings "aren't needed when profiling is integral to the core service" ([ICO, standard 12](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/12-profiling/)). *Interpretation:* the echo may be integral to the game, but research reuse is not.

**In the US** there is no federal privacy law. The relevant rules are: **California.** The CPPA's rules on automated decision-making technology took effect on 1 January 2026. Duties for significant decisions apply from 1 January 2027 ([CPPA, 23 Sep 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html)). **COPPA.** The amended rule requires separate verifiable parental consent before children's data are disclosed to third parties ([FTC, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)). **NYC Local Law 144.** It requires annual bias audits for hiring tools. The State Comptroller found its enforcement "ineffective" ([DLA Piper, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-au/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)), and only 18 of 391 employers studied posted audits ([Wright et al., FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399)). **Colorado.** Its AI Act was delayed to 30 June 2026 ([Colorado SB25B‑004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004)). Its current status is UNVERIFIED. **FTC.** It opened an inquiry into AI companion chatbots ([FTC, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)). **Likeness laws.** These protect voice and visual likeness, not behavioural profiles. The federal NO FAKES Act cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee on 18 June 2026 but is not law (search-excerpt-only, [Holland & Knight, Jun 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name)).

### Consent standards

**The research codes treat ECHO's gameplay as "passive data collection" that needs research-grade consent.** The 2025 ICC/ESOMAR Code requires the following ([ICC/ESOMAR Code 2025](https://community.esomar.org/uploads/public/knowledge-and-standards/codes-and-guidelines/ICCESOMAR-International-Code_English.pdf)): **Consent for observed behaviour.** "Passive data collection" means "observing, measuring or recording an individual's/person's actions or behaviour", and it "must be based on the consent of the data subject" (Art. 4(b)). **Separate consent for non-research use.** This covers any "non-research activity", such as acting on a person to change their attitudes (Art. 1(e)). **Purpose-specific consent for clients.** No personal data may pass to a client without consent "to the specific purpose" (Art. 6(a)). **Withdrawal.** Withdrawal must lead to deletion where feasible (Art. 4(c)). **Children.** Anyone 12 or under counts as a child, and parental consent is required.

The UK MRS goes further. Practitioners "must obtain participant consent for any personal data used to enhance AI and related technologies, including in the creation of synthetic data models" ([MRS AI guidance, item 23](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/AI+RelatedTechnologies_MRSGuidance.pdf)).

**The Stanford 1,000-person study is the practical consent benchmark** ([arXiv 2411.10109 v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10109v1.pdf)): **Review.** The team worked with its review board "for over six months". **Wording.** Participants were told their data "would be used to develop AI models simulating human behaviors", and that "achieving complete anonymity remains challenging". **Payment.** They were paid $60 and then $30, plus a bonus of up to $10. **Withdrawal.** Removal requests are honoured "for the first 25 years". **Access.** Aggregate answers are open. Individual-level access is restricted, audited and non-commercial.

A free-to-play game that "pays" in gameplay is a different exchange. The GDPR test of whether consent is freely given makes it material that research reuse is genuinely optional.

## Where a behaviour-grounded twin company would fit

Every company in this report can be placed on two axes: what its agents are built from, and who gets to see the twin. Placed that way, the map shows one empty cell. The names come from the company sections above.

| Agents built from | Synthetic populations (B2B) | One-to-one twins sold to businesses | Twins the modelled person and their friends can see |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM priors plus demographics | Ask Rally, Synthetic Users, Evidenza, Yabble, Subconscious | — | Persona and companion apps (not twins of anyone) |
| What people **say**: interviews, surveys, documents | Electric Twin audiences, Qualtrics synthetic panels | Simile, Semilattice, Fairgen Twins, YouGov Parallax, Toluna, Ipsos, Auxos, Brox | Delphi, Viven, Tavus, Selfie: "talk to my twin", never validated |
| What people **do** in the wild: transactions, analytics, outcomes | Aaru, Studio, Promi, NIQ BASES, Artificial Societies' Rothko | Simile's stated next step (transactions, RCTs, real-stakes experiments) | Haven (personal agent; no social loop) |
| What people **do** in a designed environment: choices in a world | Project Sid (LLM agents, no real people); Centaur (lab tasks, academic) | **Empty** | **Empty** |

**ECHO's slot is the bottom-right: one-to-one echoes built from choices that consenting people make in a designed world, checked against the same people's later choices, with the person and their friends as competing predictors.** The independent evidence says this is where the category's failures sit: **Individuation:** twins correlate with people at only about 0.2 ([arXiv 2509.19088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19088)). **Variance:** twin answers were too uniform in 154 of 164 outcomes ([arXiv 2509.19088 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19088v3)). **Subgroups:** Pew's errors run up to 16 points ([Pew, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really.md)). **Validation:** most tests compare synthetic answers with stated answers, so they inherit the say–do gap. Hypothetical willingness to pay overstates real payment by 21% ([Schmidt & Bijmolt, 2020](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6?fields=title,year,venue,abstract)).

The best positive result in the field, Centaur, came from training on observed choices rather than self-reports ([Binz et al., Nature, 2 Jul 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4)). Self-report grounding, by contrast, appears to hit a ceiling: the Park revision found that interviews and surveys add only 8–12 normalised points over demographics ([arXiv 2411.10109 v3, 28 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)).

A designed world offers four things that transaction data cannot: scenarios that can be repeated, giving a within-person test–retest yardstick; options that do not yet exist in the market; social context, through the villages; and a built-in human baseline, in the friends' guesses.

That is the scientific case. It is a hypothesis, because nobody has run the head-to-head comparison.

**Commercially, the research use fits best as a behaviour-first, consented panel and validation layer, not as a head-on rival.** Simile has $300M, an interview corpus and Gallup ([BI/Yahoo, 27 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-startup-raised-300-million-040101685.html)). The incumbents own 81-million-member panels and can check twins against live panellists in 30 minutes ([Toluna](https://tolunacorporate.com/ai-and-innovation/toluna-synthetic-personas/); [YouGov Parallax](https://yougov.com/business/products/parallax)). Behavioural grounding is already their stated differentiator.

What ECHO could sell that they lack is a revealed-choice ground truth. It would be a benchmark and a data asset in which every echo can be checked against what the same person later did. The buyers would pay from the research-software and AI-tooling line, competing for a share of the ~US$35bn of full-service research ([Research World, 4 Nov 2025](https://researchworld.com/articles/inside-the-153bn-insights-industry)) and the US$1–3bn of sample spend ([IA 2024 report](https://ia.insightsassociation.org/hubfs/IA-2024-Annual-Industry-Report_FINALv2-Approved-20240723.pdf?hsLang=en); [Inex One, 23 Oct 2024](https://inex.one/blog/b2b-and-healthcare-surveys-how-we-sized-the-market)), inside budgets that are flat. If the asset proves out, the natural acquirers are the CRM, customer-experience and research incumbents now buying "grounded twins", as Salesforce did with Listen Labs.

**The consumer loop fits as the engine that produces that asset, not as a standalone social app.** No product found scores friends' guesses against a person's twin. Candle's couple prompts and Spotify's "Wrapped Party" are the nearest neighbours. The friend-graph record says such loops peak in weeks and die in months unless they have a cadence or a utility. So the loop's job is to recruit and retain consenting players, generate repeated choices per person, and supply the human baseline that makes "your echo knows you better than your friends" a measurable claim.

**Regulation can be turned into positioning.** The defensible configuration has six parts: inputs limited to discrete choices, with no biometric or fine motor telemetry; separate, optional research consent; an adults-only research pool; sensitive axes excluded, or behind explicit consent; contracts that ban hiring, credit, insurance, education and electoral uses; and ESOMAR-style disclosure of validation metrics.

This is less invasive than twins that ingest email or chat logs. It also avoids each element of the Cambridge Analytica pattern.

## Biggest objections an investor would raise

**1. "Choices in a game are still stated preferences."** The Moral Machine's own authors classify hypothetical in-game choices as "stated preferences" ([Awad et al., Nature, 4 Mar 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e57f82eb306fc38c7637f33/t/5ed51626710a624d85580d53/1616616961787/life-and-death-decisions.pdf)). Their critics showed that the format of a choice can manufacture the preference it records: 87.7% saved women when forced to choose, but 97.9% chose equal treatment when allowed to ([Bigman & Gray, Nature, 4 Mar 2020](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e57f82eb306fc38c7637f33/t/5ed51626710a624d85580d53/1616616961787/life-and-death-decisions.pdf)). The best game-to-world validation found reaches about r = 0.5, and that was for navigation skill, not values ([PLOS ONE, 18 Mar 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213272)). Competitors reach for real stakes instead: Simile has delivered purchases in "virtual experiments" ([Latent Space, Aug 2026](https://latent.space/p/simile/)), and Aaru trains on transactions ([Fortune, 17 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/aaru-cofounder-ned-koh-ai-startup-sales-pitch-do-not-trust-us/)). Either could argue it is closer to real behaviour than a free game in which choices cost nothing.

**2. "Individual grounding barely beats demographics, so why would choices?"** Interviews and surveys lift Park et al.'s agents only from 74% to 82–86% of test–retest consistency ([arXiv 2411.10109 v3, 28 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)). The *Science Advances* mega-study found twins "only modestly more accurate than… a homogeneous base LLM" ([arXiv 2509.19088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19088)). If grounding in self-reports hits a ceiling, grounding in choices might hit one too. No published study compares the two on the same people.

**3. "Your players are not the population buyers need."** Volunteer game samples skew young, male and educated ([Nature, 24 Oct 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0637-6)). The Moral Machine was reweighted only for its US sample, and engagement concentrates in a small core (about 29,000 of 1.9 million EVE participants took part in all three phases; [EVE Online, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/the-first-decade-of-project-discovery)). Pew concludes there is "no substitute for rigorous, multimode, probability-based surveys" ([Pew, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really.md)). The mega-study found twins already more accurate for people with more education and income ([arXiv 2509.19088 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19088v3)), the opposite of what buyers of hard-to-reach audiences want.

**4. "You will not collect enough choices per person."** In Youyou et al., a computer needed about 70 Likes to beat a friend and 300 to beat a spouse ([Youyou et al., PNAS, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313801)). Friend-network apps lost most of their users within one to six months of peaking ([Marketing Dive, 30 Nov 2017](https://marketingdive.com/news/facebooks-tbh-app-fades-quickly-in-rankings/511944); [TechCrunch, 19 Oct 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago)). A 3D world is expensive to build and has to retain people long enough to produce those choices.

**5. "Incumbents and Simile already own grounding and validation."** YouGov checks its twins against live panellists within 30 minutes ([YouGov Parallax](https://yougov.com/business/products/parallax)). NIQ grounds its synthetic respondents in purchase panels ([NIQ, 15 Apr 2025](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2025/niq-unveils-bases-ai-screener-helping-cpg-brands-thrive-in-the-age-of-hyper-innovation)), and Kantar in years of behavioural panel data ([Kantar, 8 Aug 2024](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/AI/Synthetic-Data-The-Real-Deal)). Simile has $300M ([BI/Yahoo, 27 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-startup-raised-300-million-040101685.html)) and says it is moving to transactions, RCTs and real-stakes experiments ([Latent Space, Aug 2026](https://latent.space/p/simile/)). Salesforce now owns "digital twins… grounded in real customer behavior" ([Salesforce, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-listen-labs/)).

On this view, "behavioural" is a feature these players can add, not a moat.

**6. "Buyers will not pay for accuracy."** a16z reports that CMOs accept outputs "at least 70% as accurate" as consultants' work ([a16z, 3 Jun 2025](https://a16z.com/ai-market-research/)). Research budgets are flat or lagging: Gartner puts marketing spend at 7.8% of revenue (search-excerpt-only, [Gartner, 11 May 2026](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities)), GRIT found only 34% of buyers with management support for budget increases ([Greenbook, 18 Jun 2025](https://greenbook.org/insights/artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning/the-sea-change-insights-from-the-2025-grit-business-outlook)), and Cint's CEO says budgets lag 2024 levels ([Cint, 19 Feb 2026](https://www.mfn.se/a/cint-group/cint-group-year-end-report-2025)). Self-serve synthetic tools cost $20 to $1,799-plus a month ([askrally.com](https://askrally.com/); [semilattice.ai](https://semilattice.ai/)). The category's valuations track pedigree and distribution more than published accuracy, as Aaru's dual-priced round shows ([TechCrunch, 3 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/03/why-ai-startups-are-selling-the-same-equity-at-two-different-prices/)).

**7. "Game behaviour measures skill, not values."** In the 2022 systematic review, games designed to measure personality measured cognitive ability instead ([Frontiers in Psychology, 28 Sep 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.952002/full)). Players optimise for rewards ([Vice, 23 Jun 2016](https://www.vice.com/en/article/eve-online-project-discovery)). The flagship of game-based assessment, pymetrics, scored 4.2M applications ([Bommasani et al., 26 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27371)) without ever publishing evidence that its games predict anything.

**8. "Consent and privacy could stop it."** An echo is profiling and personal data. Choices on values axes can reveal Art. 9 special categories, whatever ECHO intends. Children's profiles are the highest-risk configuration. Erasure has to reach the model. An industry newsletter calls consent "the likeliest hard stop" for the category ([Trends.vc, 3 Sep 2026](https://trends.vc/synthetic-audiences-verified-humans-panel-fraud-consent-gap/)). Replika's €5M fine (search-excerpt-only, [CMS Enforcement Tracker](https://cms.law/en/fra/publication/gdpr-enforcement-tracker-report/italy)) shows regulators examining the legal basis for training on user data. NGL's $5M settlement ([NBC News, 9 Jul 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ftc-bans-anonymous-messaging-app-ngl-for-minors-rcna160897)) and the Sendit complaint ([FTC, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-alleges-sendit-app-its-ceo-unlawfully-collected-personal-data-children-deceived-users-about)) show what happens to social loops that fake activity or charge for curiosity.

**9. "These are two businesses, and neither is proven."** A consumer social game and a B2B research product have different customers, metrics and consent regimes. The precedents all point toward feature-level exits. Friend-graph apps were bought at their peak for talent and then shut. The one game-psychographics vendor found, Solsten, stayed inside gaming, and its latest recorded deal is a small crowdfunding round. Game-based assessment ended as features of HR suites, sold on undisclosed terms.

## What evidence would answer them

ECHO's own validation study, working title "Who Knows You Best?", is in progress. Friends guess a person's choices, and the echo is tested on the person's later visits. The table sets out, for each objection, what evidence would answer it and what that study would have to show to do so. **This report makes no claim about the study's results.**

| Objection | Evidence that would answer it | What "Who Knows You Best?" would have to show |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Game choices are stated preferences** | A subsample whose in-world choices are linked to a consequential behaviour outside the game: incentivised or real-stakes choices, or consented purchase records. Report the correlation against the Sea Hero Quest benchmark (r = 0.46–0.57, [PLOS ONE, 18 Mar 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0213272)) | That the echo predicts at least one pre-specified behaviour outside the game, not only the next in-game choice, at a pre-registered threshold |
| **2. Individual grounding adds little** | A pre-registered, held-out comparison on the same people. Baselines: a demographics-only persona, a base LLM with no persona, a twin built from the same people's survey answers, and a population-average predictor. Metrics, never just one: individual accuracy normalised by each person's own test–retest consistency (the Park et al. convention), raw accuracy, distributional error, the spread of the echo's answers relative to the person's, and subgroup error | That the echo built from behaviour beats every baseline on the person's later visits by more than its confidence interval. It must keep the real spread of answers rather than winning by predicting the most common choice, which is how the mega-study's twins failed |
| **3. Unrepresentative players** | Sample composition reported against census benchmarks, plus a quota subsample recruited through a panel partner and run through the same world | That accuracy holds across age, gender, education and gaming experience. This is the subgroup test that Pew's twins and the mega-study's twins failed |
| **4. Too few choices per person** | Learning curves of echo accuracy against the number of choices observed. Retention cohorts (day 1, 7 and 30), share of players returning for a third visit, and cost per consented, retained player | That the point where the echo overtakes friends falls within the number of choices a typical player actually makes. The Youyou et al. thresholds (70 Likes to beat a friend, 300 to beat a spouse) are the reference ([Youyou et al., PNAS, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313801)) |
| **5. Incumbents own grounding** | A blinded head-to-head on a buyer's real question against an existing synthetic product. It should be judged against a later real outcome by a partner that is neither investor nor alliance member, unlike the EY–Aaru arrangement | That echoes add the most where the say–do gap is known to be large: loyalty versus actual retention, willingness to pay versus purchase, and the socially undesirable dimensions where the Moral Machine found choices and stated views diverging |
| **6. Buyers will not pay for accuracy** | Paid pilots, renewal intent, and at least one documented decision that changed because of the accuracy gain. Unit economics set against the $20 to $1,799-plus a month that self-serve tools charge ([askrally.com](https://askrally.com/); [semilattice.ai](https://semilattice.ai/)) | That the accuracy margin is large enough to change a decision on a question a buyer actually asked |
| **7. Skill, not values** | Correlations between choice-based axes and a short cognitive measure and established value scales. Stability of each axis across visits. An ablation comparing choices made with and without in-game rewards | That the axes are stable within a person, distinct from skill, and not driven by rewards |
| **8. Consent and privacy** | A completed data-protection impact assessment. A separate research consent with a measured opt-in rate and no penalty for declining. Age assurance. Sensitive axes excluded or opt-in. A demonstrated deletion of an echo and its derived parameters. Client contracts barring Annex III uses and decisions about individuals. A disclosure pack meeting ICC/ESOMAR Arts. 7(e) and 9(b) | That adults opt into research use at a viable rate when it is genuinely optional, and that friends see predictions only on axes the person has released |
| **9. Two unproven businesses** | Funnel metrics linking the two uses: invitations sent per player, the share of friends who play, the share of players who give research consent, and return rates under a fixed cadence like Wordle's or Wrapped's | That the social loop produces consented, repeat-visit data at a cost below buying equivalent panel sample |

**What would persuade a sceptical investor is the method, as much as the numbers.** Five conditions matter most: **Pre-registration and a held-out test.** Pre-register the analysis, as Electric Twin's benchmark and the Columbia mega-study did, and test only on later visits the echo never saw. **Behavioural ground truth.** Score against what the person actually chose, not against their self-report. Self-report favours the self in a "who knows you best" contest and inherits the say–do gap ECHO claims to close. **One shared yardstick.** Put the person, their friends, the echo, a demographics-only persona and a base LLM on the same scale: the share of the person's own consistency, with confidence intervals. **Every metric reported.** Report all the metrics, including the failures. Prolific warns that judging on one metric "rewards exactly the wrong methods" ([Research Live, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/synthetic-cant-substitute-measurement-finds-study/id/5152227)). **An outside check.** Invite an independent replication or audit, in the way Gallup is validating Simile, and publish the method openly enough that ICC/ESOMAR-bound buyers can disclose it to their own clients.

A study that clears those bars would be the first evidence in this market that revealed choices make better twins than stated answers, a claim several competitors now make and none has shown.
